I first heard about La Zone 23 while I was in Fredericton. My friend Chris, who lives in the neighbourhood, had nothing but good things to say about it, but what caught my attention was the menu: Algerian and Mexican food under one roof. I’d heard the couple behind the restaurant wanted both of their culinary backgrounds to share a menu, and that’s the kind of story that gets me excited about a new place.

On a Wednesday evening in November, I made the easy trip from my place on the 85 to Sandy Hill, where a commercial strip of restaurants and grocery stores sits not far from the LRT. It was unusually warm for the month.

The restaurant is bright, with four tables and a kitchen in view of the dining area. Spanish and Arabic pop music alternated over the speakers, murals and photographs of both countries covered the walls, and a large television near the window played CBC. Soccer jerseys from Algeria and Mexico hung from the ceiling, both in shades of green.

One piece of art stood out. A mural of a Day of the Dead skull had famous buildings from Algiers in its eye sockets, a clever detail that joined the two cultures without feeling forced.

Having travelled to both Mexico and Algeria in recent years, I was eager to try the food. Before I’d settled into my seat, I smelled birria coming from the kitchen. A diner nearby was already eating a plate of it, which seemed like a good hint about what to order.

The menu mixes Mexican favourites with Algerian specialties, and prices are reasonable: burgers start at about $9 and several items cost less than $15. The quesabirria, at about $24, is the most expensive item, described as “tender fall apart slow cooked beef, cheese, onions, cilantro, served with a side of birria consommé for dipping.” On the Algerian side, I was most curious about the Algerian-style French tacos.

We ordered both, with merguez as the protein in the tacos. The fries inside were hot and steaming, crunchy outside and soft within. The merguez had the distinctive musky spice that makes the sausage so good, and with the potatoes and other fillings the whole thing reminded me of a proper American-style breakfast burrito. Sergio, who was dining with me, bit into a particularly spicy piece, a reminder of how much flavour was packed in.

![Algerian-style French tacos at La Zone 23 are stuffed with merguez sausage and hot, crispy fries](/uploads/img_4036.jpeg "La Zone 23 Algerian tacos")

The quesabirria was a highlight, too. The slow-cooked beef, melted cheese and consommé made for a satisfying combination, and it was easy to see why the diner beside me looked so happy with his order. Birria is no longer rare in Ottawa, but I still get excited when I see it on a menu.

After the meal, I spoke with Marie, who runs the restaurant with her husband, Abdel. He wasn’t there that evening. Marie is Mexican and Abdel is Algerian, and the menu reflects both of their backgrounds.

The idea came from a simple problem. “We are a Muslim couple,” Marie said, and they wanted to enjoy Mexican food that was also halal. They decided to open a restaurant serving both of their cuisines, with a fully halal menu so customers wouldn’t have to worry about cross-contamination between halal and non-halal ingredients.

The restaurant has been open for almost a year and has grown mostly by word of mouth. Marie said they didn’t invest heavily in social media at first and relied on customers telling their friends and family. The Algerian community was particularly supportive early on.

Running a small restaurant hasn’t always been easy. Marie summed up the first year simply: “I am still standing.”

She said she has her family try dishes before they go on the menu, then makes adjustments based on their feedback. She said they try to keep the food close to its roots, a balancing act with two cuisines that each have their own ingredients and techniques.

Looking ahead, Marie would like to expand and eventually spend less time working so she can spend more time with her children. For now, La Zone 23 is another baby that needs plenty of attention, and seeing customers enjoy the food makes the hard work worthwhile.

La Zone 23 is also closer to the core than most places we feature. A number of readers have told us recently that they’d like to see more spots near the centre of the city. Eat the Strip has focused on restaurants beyond the usual downtown dining circuit, but a neighbourhood place that’s easy to reach by transit is worth including.

Of the two dishes, I liked the Algerian French tacos best. They’re hearty, flavourful and nothing like what most Ottawa diners picture when they hear the word tacos, and having visited Algeria recently, I was happy to find that style of food here. The quesabirria was good, too, although at about $24 it’s the priciest item on the menu.

La Zone 23 is a family-run restaurant with a story behind its menu, and the mix of Algerian and Mexican food is worth seeking out if you want something outside the usual Ottawa rotation. At the time of writing, it’s open until 11 p.m., which makes it a good option for an evening meal. If you go, order the Algerian French tacos.
